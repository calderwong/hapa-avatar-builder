## Checkpoint 3 — optical sub-agent proposals and human validation

- **Objective:** seed Quality, description, categorization, Avatar relationships, Hapa references, and Director Notes through real vision-capable sub-agent inspection, then let humans validate/edit/append/reject without promoting agent guesses to truth.
- **Evidence inspected:** the Media / Embodiment capability page; Avatar/video node profiles; current Human Enricher identity and persistence contracts; System Media frame manifests; Avatar, song, scene, item, Tarot, and media stores; Echo claim/review patterns; four sub-agent packets covering 16 videos and 48 inspected keyframes.
- **What worked:** two vision-capable sub-agents used `view_image` on every assigned frame across four bounded waves and authored timestamp-specific observations. Their pass caught a probable duplicate pair, challenged an incorrect Calder link, found high-confidence Blue identity matches, retained uncertainty where identity was weak, rejected an incoherent sushi/fire/eye transition, and held real-person footage for consent/privacy review.
- **What was noise:** existing OCR, filenames, similarity scores, document kinds, and semantic rankers can help navigation but cannot count as optical review or author grades. They are excluded from completion evidence.
- **What was misunderstood:** three representative frames are the minimum, not a universal maximum. Every registered frame must be delivered and addressed; records with incomplete frame evidence remain unfinished.
- **What changed:** agent proposals now live in a separate revisioned store with hash-bound frame observations, explicit reviewer/task/model receipt, confidence, source-labeled inferences, and append-only events. The Human Enricher exposes filmstrips and editable Use/Append/Reject actions. Only a saved human revision projects to Echo.
- **What remains reusable:** stable Human Enricher video keys, System Media frame manifests, local atomic/event stores, existing human optimistic revisions, and exact Hapa source paths. The queue is resumable across the 10,884-record catalog.
- **New guardrail:** automation may prepare evidence but never make semantic judgments. Report exact reviewed/pending/blocked/remaining counts and never imply the full catalog was seen from queue enumeration.

**Candidate:** Flow explainer / Lesson Card — “Frames delivered and addressed: optical agent proposal to human-authored direction.” The originating Codex turn and sub-agent task receipts remain the attributed evidence.

## Checkpoint 5 — calibrated defaults, autosave, and explicit visual references

- **Objective:** use human Quality edits to calibrate later agent guidance, remove unnecessary save friction, begin untouched records at the agent grade, require explicit visible Hapa/Echo reference comparison, and measure the full-catalog campaign.
- **Evidence inspected:** 15 paired human/agent Quality grades from the local enrichment and proposal stores; two fresh three-frame, vision-model review packets; the queue/API contract; and the Human Enricher editing lifecycle.
- **What worked:** the human sample produced a 66.7% exact-match rate, 93.3% within one grade, 0.4 mean absolute error, and a +0.133 average human-minus-agent delta. Agent grade 4 was confirmed exactly in all seven paired cases; smaller lower-grade groups remain weak evidence and are exposed with their sample counts.
- **What was noise:** filenames, OCR, similarity scores, and a pre-existing reference label are not proof of a visual Hapa/Echo match. They remain retrieval aids only.
- **What was misunderstood:** the previous “References” list did not guarantee that an agent looked for visible Echo/Hapa similarities. A dedicated completed pass with observed signals, match types, citations, and negative findings is now required for new proposals.
- **What changed:** Quality now starts at the agent proposal without creating human truth; edits autosave after 350 ms and flush before row navigation; the UI and API expose aggregate calibration; new proposals require `visualReferenceAnalysis`; and the workbench renders that analysis separately from manually editable references.
- **Measured campaign evidence:** two fresh optical reviews took 302.000 and 214.738 seconds, averaging 258.369 seconds per video. Projected across 10,884 logical records, this is 781.1 serial agent-hours or 10.85 continuous days with three agents; a 20% operations allowance produces a 13.0-day plan. Exact token telemetry was unavailable, so the documented planning envelope is 130.6–272.1 million total tokens (195.9 million midpoint), with about 47.3 million output tokens implied by the measured packet sizes.
- **Verification:** the focused Human Enricher suites pass, the production build passes, and the live 8797 UI renders all 16 reviewed rows, 15 calibration pairs, the agent-started unscored Q4 record, Autosave On, no manual Save button, and the dedicated reference panel. The repository-wide run reports 1,655 passes and two unrelated known failures: a stale Echo transition visual digest and stale Record Garden verification ledger. The Tarot desktop smoke aborted at Electron launch; this checkout does not define a `smoke:overcard` package script.
- **What remains reusable:** revisioned human overlays, append-only receipts, the resumable evidence queue, registered frame manifests, per-grade calibration bins, and explicit Hapa source citations.
- **New guardrail:** human calibration is a bounded prior, not a score rewrite. Sample counts travel with every aggregate, direct frame inspection remains mandatory, and no dedicated reference scan is represented as “no match.”

**Candidate:** Lesson Card — “Agent-default, human-delta media review with explicit visual-reference evidence.” The originating Codex turn and timed sub-agent receipts remain the attributed evidence.

## Checkpoint 6 — split optical and bounded-Card reference workers

- **Objective:** prevent optical workers from repeatedly searching the full Hapa knowledge base, make reference models/setups independently replaceable, and restrict reference claims to existing Hapa/Echo Card material.
- **Evidence inspected:** the combined optical proposal contract; its queue instructions; the Human Enricher proposal UI; the 2,391-record item/Card store; the 74 Avatar Cards; the 157 Song Cards; and the 85 source-backed Echo reference catalog records.
- **What worked:** frame inspection, quality, description, categories, tags, and visible-signal capture are reusable without any lore lookup. The 85-reference catalog is a stable bounded corpus; the optional Echo-core packet contains exactly 316 records (85 references, 157 songs, 74 Avatars).
- **What was noise:** having each optical worker traverse Avatar, song, scene, item, and Tarot stores. A two-video Terra Max benchmark consumed 2,097,910 input tokens largely because of repeated raw-store traversal.
- **What was misunderstood:** the 85 Echo reference records are source-backed `hapa.song-reference-catalog.v1` entries in `hapa-songs-store.json`; they are not durable Universal Card-plane records in `item-manager-store.json`. The new packet is an attributable Reference Card projection and does not pretend Card custody was already minted.
- **What changed:** new optical submissions reject Card/Avatar/song/scene/lore matches and emit only visible reference signals. A separate reference-review store depends on an exact optical revision and hash-bound Card packet, records model/reasoning/parameters independently, and rejects any Card ID outside the packet. The UI, API, and CLI show and operate the two passes separately; accepted human enrichment preserves both lineages.
- **Verification:** 13 focused Human Enricher tests pass; the production build passes; live loopback probes returned 85 default packet Cards, 316 Echo-core Cards, and all 16 completed optical records in the new needs-reference queue.
- **New guardrail:** reference workers may rank or reject supplied Cards only. They may not invent candidates, search raw knowledge stores, or represent projected reference records as durable Card-plane custody.

**Candidate:** Decision Card / Flow explainer — “Optical signal handoff to bounded Hapa Card reference workers.” The originating Codex turn remains the attributed source.

## Checkpoint 8 — Terra-medium stage parallelism and palette hex values

- **Objective:** measure real Terra-medium throughput for the separated optical/reference stages, determine practical concurrency, and add most-used palette hex values to future optical proposals.
- **Evidence inspected:** three videos with nine SHA-256-bound first/middle/last frames; three `gpt-5.6-terra` medium workers; the canonical 258-Card `echo-reference-cards` packet at Reference Atlas run `fnv1a:13ae2982`; all six worker timing receipts; the optical proposal normalizer, queue instructions, UI, and focused tests.
- **What worked:** all optical workers used three direct image calls and produced per-frame observations plus 3–8 visually estimated palette colors. The reference stage kept image calls at zero, used only the supplied packet, preserved negative findings, and reached three-way overlap for 53 seconds.
- **What was noise:** claiming three-way optical throughput merely because three child tasks were requested. Their recorded image-inspection intervals were serialized. Pixel histograms and automated color extraction were also excluded because the requested palette belongs to the human-facing visual judgment, not a colorimetric measurement.
- **Measured result:** optical agent times were 10, 9, and 11 seconds, but the batch took 38 seconds and had peak overlap one. Reference times were 72, 122, and 96 seconds; the batch took 131 seconds, reached peak overlap three, and achieved 2.21× effective speedup over its 290 seconds of represented serial work. In this runtime, capacity planning should use one effective optical lane feeding three reference lanes.
- **Projection:** for 10,884 videos, the narrow observed-batch estimates are 38.3 hours for optical and 132.0 hours for references. A continuously filled three-worker reference queue projects to 97.4 hours. Pipelining is therefore reference-bound at roughly 4.1–5.5 days before frame preparation, failures, persistence, and human review; separate full-catalog stages total about 7.1 days under observed batch behavior.
- **What changed:** new optical submissions require 3–8 uppercase `#RRGGBB` colors with a label, prevalence, explanation, and exact frame hashes. The Human Enricher renders labeled swatches and uncertainty. Queue/API/docs distinguish visual estimates from automated or colorimetric truth. Full methodology is in `docs/HUMAN_ENRICHER_TERRA_MEDIUM_BENCHMARK.md`.
- **New guardrail:** requested agent slots are not evidence of effective parallelism. Report peak observed overlap, batch wall time, and effective concurrency separately for every stage.

**Candidate:** Lesson Card — “One vision feeder, three bounded-reference workers: measure stage concurrency instead of assuming it.” The originating Codex turn and six Terra-medium worker receipts remain the attributed evidence.

## Checkpoint 9 — move palette authority from video to frame

- **Objective:** correct the optical palette boundary so First, Mid, and Last frames are described independently instead of flattening their colors into one video-wide palette.
- **What was misunderstood:** the Terra-medium benchmark prompt asked for recurring/dominant colors “across the frames.” That can erase deliberate palette transitions and produce colors that never coexist in any single image.
- **What changed:** every normalized optical frame now owns its own palette receipt, exact parent frame hash, 3–8 distinct uppercase `#RRGGBB` colors, labels, prevalence, explanations, and uncertainty. New submissions fail if any inspected First/Mid/Last frame lacks its own complete palette. Aggregate proposal palettes are no longer normalized. The UI renders swatches and values inside each frame caption, while queue instructions explicitly set `scope: per-inspected-frame` and `wholeVideoPaletteForbidden: true`.
- **Compatibility:** all 16 legacy optical records remain readable with empty per-frame palettes. They require a fresh optical revision before palette data can be claimed; the system does not fabricate or migrate a whole-video estimate into frame evidence.
- **New guardrail:** colors from different timestamps may be compared by a later human or reference worker, but the optical pass never merges them into one authoritative video palette.

**Candidate:** Correction Card — “The palette belongs to the frame that visibly contains it.” The Human correction and originating Codex turn remain the attributed source.

## Checkpoint 10 — trusted rolling optical campaign

- **Objective:** recheck the live Sol/Ultra/Fast environment, measure up to twenty Terra-medium vision-only jobs, replace worker-authored clocks with scheduler truth, make optical output schema-safe, and begin a resumable three-worker production campaign.
- **Evidence inspected:** the live Sol session context; an empirical fourth-child spawn rejection; 20 Terra-medium rollout files; 60 exact `view_image` calls; per-session token counters; the 10,884-video Human Enricher summary; queue construction across System Media, Avatar, Tarot, scene, item, song, and Hell Week inputs; registered-frame readiness; current proposal/store/API/CLI contracts; and the Media / Embodiment plus Overwatch protocols.
- **What worked:** three Terra vision workers genuinely overlapped. Twenty jobs averaged 25.256 scheduler seconds and consumed 2,780,078 gross tokens. Every worker opened three images, and tool receipts showed no Reference Atlas, raw Hapa store, web, OCR, or semantic-heuristic access.
- **What was noise:** model-authored timestamps, the coordinator's remembered four-slot description as a substitute for a live probe, and counting `data/media-library.json` alone as the full Human Enricher catalog. Several worker timestamps were missing or false; the aggregate dashboard includes other owning stores and read-only projections.
- **What was misunderstood:** Sol Ultra/Fast changed the root session configuration but did not raise this live task above three active child workers. Twenty total jobs means rolling waves, not twenty-way concurrency. Also, “three supplied hashes” was not enough integrity: the previous submit route did not prove that those hashes belonged to the exact frame set the queue issued.
- **What changed:** a separate ignored campaign ledger now plans frame-ready work, binds stable video identity to an immutable canonical SHA-256 First/Mid/Last manifest, caps active leases at three, and recovers stale claims. Opaque client-held leases, exact manifest comparison, inode-safe owner-token/PID-checked cross-process locks, frozen per-claim configuration, and immutable job/attempt/frame/lease-bound local receipts prevent stale or drifting callers from settling work. Manual submissions and campaign planning share video-custody exclusion. Completion keeps campaign custody across prepared state, proposal persistence, and final settlement; reconciliation freshly checks the review store under that lock, finalizes a match, or requeues an expired reviewless preparation as retryable. Successful retries are idempotent. Agent submissions cannot supply final human status or trusted campaign lineage, and only the human decision endpoint can decide a proposal. The strict adapter accepts structural aliases only: it rejects unknown/Card/reference/relationship fields, common explicit semantic-reference assertions in free text, partial or contradictory frame mappings, bare palette hex arrays, missing prevalence/uncertainty/evidence, synthesized semantic defaults, and whole-video palettes while allowing literal visible Hapa/Echo lettering. Manual optical queue packets omit prior mixed-pass reviews. Unknown store schemas, malformed roots/collections/statuses/lease timestamps, and duplicate custody identities fail closed instead of disappearing during normalization. Initial FNV jobs remain readable but never rely on that digest alone. API, CLI, UI status, node discovery, benchmark documentation, and append-only receipts expose the same campaign truth.
- **Production kickoff:** 100 verified frame-ready records entered the queue. Three concurrent Terra-medium workers personally opened nine total frames and submitted three schema-valid pending-human proposals with Quality 4, 3, and 4. Their runtime receipts captured 44.942, 69.343, and 50.606 seconds plus 433,158 aggregate gross tokens; every receipt records exactly three direct image-view calls. The live dashboard now reports 19 agent-reviewed/pending-human records and 10,865 remaining. The richer accepted schema projects materially slower than the earlier compact benchmark, so the production receipt—not the lower compact-packet estimate—is the safer evolving forecast.
- **What remains reusable:** the existing full-catalog dashboard, stable human video keys, registered frame manifests, revisioned proposal store, human calibration prior, autosaving Human Enricher, and independent Reference Pass remain owners of their prior responsibilities.
- **New guardrail:** runtime settings, concurrency, catalog size, timing, tokens, and frame binding are measured from their owning receipts. Never trust model prose for scheduler facts, never checkpoint mutable offsets as identity, and never dispatch an unprepared logical video as though its frames were optically reviewed.

**Candidate:** Flow explainer / Decision Card — “Digest-bound rolling vision workers with coordinator-observed, locally bound receipts.” The originating Codex turn, campaign event log, and Terra rollout sessions remain the attributed evidence.
